Privacy & End-to-End Encryption

Privacy is a human right. OpenTrawl is built in the EU 🇪🇺, open source under the MIT licence, and designed so you never have to take our word for it: today everything stays on your Mac, and our planned sync and cloud tiers are end-to-end encrypted so even we cannot read your data.

Local + local models

Pair OpenTrawl on your Mac with a local coding agent running Ollama or MLX. Neither OpenTrawl nor any AI company sees your messages, emails, or notes.

End-to-end encrypted (planned)

On planned sync and cloud tiers, your archive is encrypted on your device before upload. Cloud storage holds only ciphertext it cannot open.

Hardware-locked memory (planned)

When your phone or cloud agent searches your archive while your Mac sleeps, queries run inside hardware-locked memory that requires your agent's live key. Even OpenTrawl admins cannot read RAM or dump your archive.

Verify what runs on your Mac and in the cloud

On your Mac today: Open OpenTrawl and use Click to copy AI review prompt before granting Full Disk Access. It gives Claude or Codex the signed app bundle on your disk and its public GitHub commit so your AI can check what the app reads and confirm there is no telemetry.
In our planned Encrypted Cloud: Every server build will come straight from our public GitHub repo and be published 72 hours before it goes live. You will be able to check from your terminal that the server handling your queries matches the exact public source code.

Where the limits are

  • Cloud AI providers see what you send them. If you connect a cloud AI (Claude, ChatGPT, Codex), whatever conversations or emails that agent opens go to that AI provider under its privacy terms. Use a local model on your Mac if you want no AI company to see your data.
  • Storage servers still see basic metadata. Encrypted cloud backups hide your messages, notes, and source names, but storage servers can still see coarse padded file sizes and when you sync. Stay on Local if you want zero cloud backups.
  • Your Mac or iPhone itself. If malware runs on an unlocked device, local files are exposed like any other app on that device.
