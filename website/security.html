Privacy & Encryption

Privacy is a human right. OpenTrawl is built in the EU 🇪🇺 and open source under the MIT licence. Today everything stays on your Mac. When we add sync and cloud access, it will be end-to-end encrypted so nobody, not us and not your cloud provider, can read your data.

Local on your Mac today

Your archives are plain SQLite files under ~/.opentrawl. No OpenTrawl servers, no account, no analytics, and no tracking.

Local models, zero AI exposure

Pair OpenTrawl with a local coding agent running Ollama or MLX on Apple Silicon. Neither OpenTrawl nor any AI company sees your messages, emails, or notes.

End-to-end encrypted (coming next)

Your archive is encrypted on your device before it ever leaves. Nobody, not us and not your cloud provider, can read your data. Only you can.

How OpenTrawl works today

  • Strictly read-only. OpenTrawl copies your history into local archives for search. It cannot send a message, delete an email, or edit a note.
  • Isolated external plugins. Every external plugin runs inside a macOS sandbox with zero network, folder, or Keychain access by default. OpenTrawl pins each installed plugin's SHA-256 hash and stops if an update changes the binary or asks for new access.
  • Verify the Mac app before granting Full Disk Access. Open OpenTrawl and use Click to copy AI review prompt. It gives Claude or Codex the signed app bundle on your disk and its public GitHub commit so your AI can check what the app reads and confirm there is no telemetry.
When sync and cloud launch: Encryption happens on your device before anything is uploaded, with your keys staying under your control. Any server handling encrypted remote search will be built from public GitHub code so you can verify what runs from your terminal.

Where the limits are

  • Cloud AI providers see what you send them. If you connect a cloud AI (Claude, ChatGPT, Codex), whatever conversations or emails that agent opens go to that AI provider under its privacy terms. Use a local model on your Mac if you want no AI company to see your data.
  • Cloud backups still expose basic metadata. Encrypted backups hide your messages, notes, and source names, but storage servers can still see coarse file sizes and when you sync. Stay local if you want zero cloud backups.
  • Your Mac or iPhone itself. If malware runs on an unlocked device, local files are exposed like any other app on that device.
